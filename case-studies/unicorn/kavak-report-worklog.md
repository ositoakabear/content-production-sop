# Case Study: Kavak Unicorn Financial Report

This document records the production workflow for the Kavak weekly unicorn financial report.

## 1. Initial Topic

Company:

- Kavak
- Mexico
- Used-car ecommerce, auto finance, and vehicle services

Initial angle:

> Latin America's highest-valued private unicorn is moving from heavy-asset expansion to profitability repair.

Core thesis:

> Kavak is not only a used-car ecommerce company. It uses self-operated vehicle sourcing and refurbishment as the trust layer, auto finance as the profit engine, and AI as the operating system that connects pricing, inventory, refurbishment, sales, and credit risk.

## 2. First Draft

The first draft was too short and worked more like a summary.

It covered:

- 2025 transaction volume
- First consolidated monthly profitability
- Kavak Capital loan growth
- Three revenue pillars
- Basic conclusion and risk points

Problem:

- Not enough depth for a WeChat long-form business analysis article
- Founder team and investor background were too thin
- AI usage was underdeveloped
- Competitor comparison was missing

## 3. Expanded Structure

The article was expanded into six major analytical blocks:

1. Founder team
2. Core financial and operational data
3. Core revenue pillars
4. Strategic conclusion
5. Competitor comparison
6. Major investor background

Later, AI was promoted into its own section because it was central to Kavak's operating model.

Final article structure:

1. Opening judgment
2. Founder team
3. Core financial and operational data
4. Revenue pillars
5. AI operating system
6. Competitor comparison
7. Strategic thinking
8. Investor background and investment logic
9. Personal commentary block
10. Final summary

## 4. Founder Team Iteration

The founder section initially only explained the founding reason.

It was improved by adding:

- Carlos García Ottati
- Loreanne García
- Roger Laughlin
- Their combined background in platform operations, local execution, people/organization, finance, and scaling

Editorial lesson:

> For founder sections, do not stop at "why the company was founded." Explain why this specific team was suited to solve this specific problem.

## 5. AI Analysis Iteration

AI was initially mentioned mainly as a pricing engine.

It was later reframed as Kavak's operating system across four endpoints:

- Sourcing: instant vehicle pricing
- Refurbishment: prioritization and cost prediction
- Sales: dynamic inventory turnover and pricing
- Finance: credit scoring and default risk control

Editorial lesson:

> AI analysis should connect directly to unit economics: purchase price, refurbishment efficiency, inventory turnover, and bad debt rate.

## 6. Competitor Comparison Iteration

The competitor comparison was built around model differences rather than only named competitors.

Comparison groups:

- Kavak: self-operated + AI + finance
- Carvana: online experience in a more mature market
- Traditional dealers: local supply but weaker standardization
- Marketplace platforms: lighter assets but weaker control

Key distinction:

> In Latin America, the main bottleneck is not traffic. It is trust and credit risk.

## 7. Investor Section Iteration

The investor section initially ended too abruptly after introducing a16z.

It was expanded into:

- a16z background
- Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz background
- a16z's operating support model
- Why Kavak fits the thesis of software and finance rebuilding traditional heavy-asset industries
- What continued investment implies
- What risks remain

Editorial lesson:

> Investor background should not be a standalone biography. It should explain why this investor's thesis matches the company and what that implies for the next phase.

## 8. WeChat Layout Iteration

The first long version was dense and read like a report.

The layout was adjusted to be more mobile-friendly:

- Shorter paragraphs
- Numbered section headers
- Light background information cards
- Green section markers
- Orange judgment boxes
- Reduced bullet density
- Clear final commentary block

Recurring series labels now standardized by the SOP:

- Series name: `每周独角兽观察`
- Commentary block: `每周独角兽观察员点评`

Purpose:

- Add voice and personality
- Provide a non-neutral editorial judgment
- Avoid ending with only factual summary

## 9. Visual Asset Production

Final asset package:

- 2.35:1 safe-crop title cover

Important correction:

The first title cover versions were separate crops. The better approach was to create a safe master composition where all important content sits in the central safe area, then export both wide and square versions without losing the main message.

- Competitor comparison

## 11. Reusable Takeaways

- Start with a sharp business question.
- Build the article around a thesis, not a company profile.
- Treat founder, AI, competitor, and investor sections as analytical blocks.
- For WeChat, format matters as much as content depth.
- For cover images, design for crop safety from the beginning.
- Avoid fake people images; use public source images or neutral visual cards.
- Every major section should end with a judgment or implication.
- Keep series naming stable as `每周独角兽观察` and `每周独角兽观察员点评`.
